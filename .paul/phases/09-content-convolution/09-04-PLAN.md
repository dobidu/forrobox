<objective>
## Goal

**Twelve new grooves**, three per regional profile, so each bank holds four instead of one. Drafted
from the four that exist and from each profile's own description, rendered to `.wav` through the
shipping chain **and** to `.mid` through 07-01's cross-checked writer, and put in front of Esmeraldo
Filho before they ship.

## Purpose

This is the plan the user asked for in the first place — *"criar mais patterns por regional
profile"*, pointing at a cycler that read `PÉ-DE-SERRA 01` while CAMPINA GRANDE was selected. Three
plans of mechanism made it possible for a groove to be one JSON object that six gates check; this
one writes the music.

**And it does not claim the music is right.** Judging whether a baião is a baião is a domain call
and Esmeraldo is the percussionist. The checkpoint is the deliverable, not a formality — the drafts
ship only if he says they should.

## Output

- `assets/profiles.json` — four banks of four
- `applyGroove` in `src/Profiles.{h,cpp}`, which finally has a caller
- `renderAuditionFiles` generalised per-groove, writing a `.wav` and a `.mid` for each, **asserted**
- A re-pinned velocity fingerprint, moved deliberately in the commit that adds the content
</objective>

<decisions>
## Four per bank, and eight of the sixteen names come from the spec

`PLANNING.md:843` lists eight rhythm labels — `PÉ-DE-SERRA 01`, `BAIÃO SECO`, `XOTE LENTO`,
`ARRASTA-PÉ`, `XAXADO 88`, `FORRÓ ELÉTRICO`, `QUADRILHA`, `PISADINHA`. Those are rhythms, and each
belongs to a region more than to the others: a xaxado is Pernambuco, a pisadinha is the modern
São Francisco sound, a pé-de-serra is Campina Grande's. They are assigned to the profile whose
description already names that music, and the remaining eight names are drafted.

**All sixteen names go to the checkpoint with the patterns.** A name is a claim about what a groove
IS, and it is exactly as much Esmeraldo's call as the notes.

## The renderer is generalised, not written

`tests/VoiceTest.cpp:4893`'s `renderAuditionFiles` already renders every profile to a `.wav` through
the shipping chain — `useShippedChain`, the real `CACHAÇA`, the limiter, four bars at the profile's
tempo plus a tail — and `TestMain.cpp:88` invokes it behind `--render-audition`. Phase 3 built it so
a person could judge whether the grooves matched the prototype. This plan points it at grooves
instead of profiles and adds the `.mid`.

## And it becomes ASSERTED, because its own docstring says it is not

It currently reads *"Not a test — nothing here asserts."* `04-01`'s law is that a checkpoint
artefact needs the same scrutiny as a test, and `ui-renders` is the counter-example in this same
repository: those PNGs are checked for their far corner and their knob ink, which is what caught a
2× render that was a 1200×780 chassis in the corner of a 2400×1560 image.

A rendered groove that is silent, clipped, or **the wrong groove** is a file Esmeraldo would judge
as if it were the draft. The last of those is the one nothing would catch by ear across sixteen
files.
</decisions>

    **The renderer, per groove.** Generalise `renderAuditionFiles` to loop each profile's

    `<profile-id>__<groove-id>.wav` / `.mid`.

    **The `.mid` beside it.** `renderStandardMidiFile (state, bpm, steps, gate)` already exists and
    is cross-checked byte for byte against the prototype's `exportMIDI`. Apply the groove to a
    `State`, call it with that groove's own bpm, write the bytes. Do NOT re-implement any part of the
    writer.

    **ASSERT the renders.** Its docstring says "nothing here asserts"; that stops being true. Each
    render must be checked for: finite samples, a peak above a silence floor, a peak at or below
    full scale, and — the one that matters — **that it is the right groove**. Decode the hit count
    out of the `.mid` just written and compare it against that groove's own velocity count from
    `decodePattern`. Sixteen files that all render CAMPINA's default would pass every other check
    and no one would hear it.

    Keep the audibility rules the file already has: `exportMIDI` excludes muted channels and ghosts,
    so the MIDI hit count is the STORED grid's, not the humanised performance's — 07-03 recorded
    those as two deliberately different data paths. If the counts cannot be made to agree exactly
    because of that, compare against the stored grid and say so in the comment rather than relaxing
    the check to an inequality.

    Avoid: asserting a wall-clock duration. Three of those were written in Phase 8 and all three
    failed on a busy machine rather than on the code.
  </action>
